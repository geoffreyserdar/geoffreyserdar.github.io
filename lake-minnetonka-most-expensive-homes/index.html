What actually makes a lake home expensive

People ask this like there’s a formula. There isn’t — but there are a handful of things that almost always show up in the price.

  • Shoreline — how many feet, and what kind. Level sand is a different house than a steep lot. A protected bay is a different house than one that takes a beating from open water. And can you actually dock a boat.
  • Which bay, and which way it faces. West and southwest light, and the coves that stay private, tend to cost more.
  • Land. A multi-lot compound is a different thing than a tight Cottagewood lot. You’re not comparing the same product.
  • Privacy. The biggest asking prices often start quietly, off the public list. It’s normal for those houses to sit for years.
  • Boat life and the extra buildings. Boathouses, guest houses, sport courts show up in the $10 million-and-up stories.
  • Taxes. Lake Point is listed around $320,000 a year. Other houses in this range carry six-figure tax bills too. That’s the real carrying cost, not a footnote.
  • The gap between asking and sold. Southways once asked $53.5 million and later got subdivided. Lake Point has come from $68 million down to $50 million and still hasn’t closed. Asking prices are marketing. Sold prices are what happened.
