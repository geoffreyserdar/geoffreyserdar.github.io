Lake Minnetonka access & boat slips

Buying near the lake is not the same as having a place to keep a boat. Frontage, deeded rights, association docks, municipal lottery/waitlists, and public launches are different doors — and most of them are slower or narrower than a listing photo implies.

Limits first. This page explains categories and cites city-published Excelsior and Wayzata rules. It does not invent openings-per-year, odds, fees as of today, or slip counts for other towns. Private marina contracts and association docs vary — read the actual instrument. For live market solds, use the weekly Closed page.

Five different kinds of “access”

1 · Frontage

Your lot touches the water

True riparian / shoreline ownership (subject to LMCD, DNR, and local dock rules). This is the rare case where “lake access” and “my dock” can mean the same thing — still not automatic for every craft size.

2 · Deeded

Rights written into the title

Sometimes a non-frontage lot carries recorded rights to a shared dock or outlot. The deed and plat control — not the listing headline. Have title read it.

3 · Association

HOA / co-op / condo dock

Rules, waitlists, fees, and transferability live in the association docs. Buying the unit ≠ automatic preference for a slip unless the docs say so.

4 · Municipal

City marina / city docks

Resident lottery or waitlist systems (examples below: Wayzata lottery; Excelsior waitlist). Near-lake address in a different city usually does not qualify.

5 · Public launch

Trailer in, trailer out

Public boat launches get you on the water for a day. They are not a seasonal slip. Congestion and parking rules are real — check the city/county site for the ramp you mean.

What this page is not

  • Not a promise that “every Excelsior / Wayzata buyer gets a slip”
  • Not invented odds or “usually X openings a year” unless a city PDF states it
  • Not a private-marina rate sheet
  • Not legal advice — confirm residency, fees, and craft limits with the city before you rely on them

City example: Wayzata municipal slips

Source of truth: City of Wayzata Boat Slips page, Ordinance Ch. 311, and Boat Slip Policy (updated Oct 2023).

  • Residents only for municipal marina permits (Code 311.04 / policy).
  • Renewals: prior holders must renew by close of business on the first Tuesday of February or risk forfeiture.
  • New permits: applications due by that same February deadline; open slips filled by lottery on the first Tuesday of March.
  • Priority Group: applicants unsuccessful for at least seven consecutive years enter a Priority Group; half of available new slips (rounding up) go to that pool first.
  • Craft limits (policy): max length 26 ft; beam ≤ 8½ ft; draft ≤ 36 in; one craft per slip under LMCD rules.
  • One permit per common address. Permits are not assignable/transferable.
  • Doors: wayzata.org/165/Boat-Slips · lottery form on city FormCenter · FAQ on city site

The policy does not publish a fixed “openings per year” number — available slips = marina capacity minus renewals that year. Don’t invent a typical count.

City example: Excelsior residential moorings

Source of truth: City of Excelsior Mooring Policy (approved 12/5/22) and published Dock Waiting List.

  • Residents can apply to the Dock Waiting List anytime via the city website.
  • Assignment priority: renewing residents first, then reassignment list, then waitlist applicants without a mooring on their property — in waitlist order.
  • Key calendar: renewals/fees by Feb 28; city contacts waitlist after March 1 to fill vacancies; craft off moorings by Oct 31.
  • Dock slips have craft limits (policy table: e.g. dock slip max length 24 ft / width 11 ft — sailboats/jet-skis not allowed at dock slips). Buoys/slides have separate rules.
  • One mooring type per household/address when a waitlist exists for that type.
  • Waitlist length: city PDF “Dock Waiting List — Excelsior Residents (UPDATED 06/10/2025)” numbers entries through 120+ — long enough that “I’ll just get a city slip after closing” is usually fantasy. Use the current PDF, not this page’s snapshot.
  • Door: excelsiormn.org — Residential Moorings

How to verify before you buy

  1. Ask which access category the seller is claiming (frontage / deeded / association / municipal / launch-only).
  2. For deeded or association: get the recorded instrument or HOA docs — don’t rely on brochure language.
  3. For municipal: open that city’s current boat-slip / mooring page + policy PDF; confirm residency rules and the live waitlist/lottery calendar.
  4. Confirm LMCD / DNR constraints for the craft you actually own.

Method & limits

Categories are structural. Wayzata and Excelsior details come from city PDFs / pages staged for this topic (policy, ordinance, waitlist revision 06/10/2025). Other lake towns have their own programs — do not assume Excelsior or Wayzata rules transfer. No invented annual opening counts.

Hostile FAQ

If I buy near the lake, do I automatically get a boat slip?

No. Near-lake, lake-town, and a usable slip are different things. Frontage, deeded rights, association docks, city lottery/waitlists, and public launches each work differently.

Can non-residents get a Wayzata municipal marina slip?

Wayzata’s ordinance and policy limit municipal marina permits to City of Wayzata residents, filled through the annual lottery for open slips. Confirm on the city’s Boat Slips page before you underwrite a purchase on that hope.

How long is Excelsior’s dock waitlist?

The city’s residential Dock Waiting List PDF dated 06/10/2025 runs past 120 numbered entries. That is a dated snapshot — use the city’s current PDF. It is not a promise of timeline.

Is a public boat launch the same as a slip?

No. A launch is day-use access. A slip (private, association, or municipal) is where the boat lives for the season under a permit or right.
