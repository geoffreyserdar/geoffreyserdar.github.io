What MN foreclosure statutes actually say

Unusual question, on purpose: not “how many days until I lose the house,” but what the published statutes and county pages actually state about foreclosure by advertisement, notice, reinstatement, and redemption. Realtor-blog math is banned here.

The unusual Q

Most “Minnesota foreclosure timeline” articles invent a single neat calendar. Minnesota’s common path — foreclosure by advertisement under Minn. Stat. ch. 580, with related rules in ch. 581 / 582 — is a stack of notices, publication weeks, service rules, reinstatement rights, and redemption periods that vary by facts. The honest answer is: read the notice + the statute + your county sheriff page.

Receipts (not invented day-counts)

LawHelpMN

“Your Rights in Foreclosure”

Consumer guide notes a common ~120-day delinquency window before many servicers start foreclosure; 6 weeks newspaper publication; personal service on occupants ≥4 weeks before sale if occupied; right to reinstate before sale; redemption often 6 or 12 months; ability to postpone the sale once (with a longer wait) that then triggers a 5-week redemption. Cite the PDF — do not treat it as your personal clock.

Hennepin Sheriff

Sales office — plain English

Governed by statutes 580, 581, and 582. Notice of sale must be published in a qualified newspaper once a week for 6 weeks. Occupants must be served at least 4 weeks before the scheduled sale. The sheriff does not keep a calendar of scheduled sales. Redemption is usually 6 months; other periods include 5 weeks, 2 months, and 12 months.

Minn. Stat. 580.30

Reinstatement before sale

If the default amount (and listed costs) is paid before the sale, the mortgage is reinstated and foreclosure stops. The holder must inform the mortgagor of the reinstatement amount within three days of a request. A quoted amount is effective for seven days or until sale, whichever comes first.

Minn. Stat. 582.032

Reduced redemption (abandoned / qualifying)

Statute allows a court order that can reduce redemption in defined cases (including certain abandoned residential premises). Notices of sale must flag that redemption may be reduced to five weeks if such an order enters. Read the section — do not assume your house qualifies.

Hostile FAQ

Can Geoffrey stop my foreclosure?

No. A realtor cannot reinstate your mortgage or cancel a sheriff sale. Talk to your servicer, a HUD-approved counselor, and an attorney when rights are at stake. Geoffrey can help you understand listing, short-sale, keep, or as-is paths once you know where you stand — see seller options.

Does Hennepin publish a calendar of upcoming foreclosure sales?

No. The Hennepin County Sheriff’s Office states it does not have a list or calendar of scheduled sales. Publication runs in a qualified newspaper for six weeks before a scheduled sale date.

Is Geoffrey a foreclosure consultant under 325N?

No. Licensed Minnesota realtor, Serdar+Partners at Compass. Chapter 325N is a different legal lane. Do not confuse a listing appointment with a 325N consulting contract.

Why won’t you just tell me my exact deadline?

Because your notice, loan type, county, and any court orders control — not a webpage. If someone guarantees your exact clock without reading your papers, treat that as a red flag.
