Life down there

The Villages is a 55-and-up community, and golf carts are how everybody gets around. There are cart paths, cart bridges and even cart tunnels, so you hop in one to grab groceries, visit a friend or head down to the square.

The town squares are the heart of it. Spanish Springs, Lake Sumter Landing and Brownwood each have something going on every night, and Sawgrass Grove is one of the newer ones. It moves at a different pace than a Minnesota neighborhood, which is exactly why I'd want you to see it before you decide anything.

Florida doesn't have a state income tax, and Minnesota does. What that means for you depends on your situation, so that's a great conversation to have with your tax person.

Questions people ask me

Should we sell first or buy first?

There's no one answer. If the Minnesota sale is paying for the Florida home, selling first keeps it simple. If you can carry both for a little while, buying first can feel a lot calmer. We'll look at your numbers and your timeline, and I'll tell you honestly which way I'd go.

What if we need both homes for a while?

That happens a lot. Some families use what lenders call a bridge loan to cover the overlap. Whether it makes sense is a question for your lender, and I'm happy to connect you with good ones.

What's a Florida homestead exemption?

If the Florida home is where you'll actually live, you can apply to the county for a homestead exemption, which lowers your property taxes on that home. It isn't automatic, so you do have to apply. Becoming a Florida resident is its own step, and your tax person is the right one to walk you through it.
