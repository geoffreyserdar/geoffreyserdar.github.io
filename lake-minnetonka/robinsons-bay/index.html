Robinson's Bay on Lake Minnetonkaand the ground where Wright's Northome stood

By Geoffrey Serdar

On the south shore of Robinson's Bay in Deephaven, right at the curve in Northome Road, Frank Lloyd Wright once designed one of his grandest houses. A newer house has stood there since 1972, and this November it sold.

Here is a little more of its story, a map of where it sits, what became of Wright's living room, and how this quiet corner of the Lower Lake fits together.

Northome, and the room that went to New York

Francis Little was a lawyer and a businessman who came from Peoria as a bank vice president, and his wife, Mary, was a talented pianist who had studied in Germany. Frank Lloyd Wright had already built them a house in Illinois. After they moved to Minneapolis in 1907, they asked him for a summer house on Lake Minnetonka, and he drew a string of low pavilions that ran about 250 feet along the shore, following two hills, with the living room up on the crest looking out at the lake.

That room was the heart of the house. Bands of leaded glass ran down both long walls, and at Francis's request Wright left the middle panes plain so the family could see the lake through them. Mary gave recitals there, and the family sometimes called it the music room.

After Mary died in 1941, their daughter Eleanor Stevenson and her family winterized the house and lived in it year round. They looked for a buyer who could save the house. When no one could, and a smaller house couldn't go up on the property until the old one came down, a group of Midwest architects and Wright fans called the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and the Met bought the house in 1972.

Curators took it apart that spring. The living room sat in storage for almost ten years, then opened at the Met in 1982 with nearly all of its original furniture. The library went to the Allentown Art Museum in Pennsylvania, and a bedroom hallway is at the Minneapolis Institute of Art, so a piece of Northome never left Minnesota.

A 1972 house on the Northome shore

Richard and Sarah Diaz bought this house in 2011. It sits high on a wooded lot on the south shore of Robinson's Bay in Deephaven, with its own dock on the water below.

The house went up in 1972, one story of stone and stucco on about an acre and a third, with a walkout lower level. That was the same year Frank Lloyd Wright's Northome, which had stood on this ground since 1914, came down.

It went on the market September 3 and sold two months later at its full asking price.

How Robinson's Bay fits together

Start with the name. The bay is named for an early settler named Robinson, who came to its shore in 1853 and served as one of Wayzata's first justices of the peace.

Wrapping around the south shore is Gibson Point. Charles Gibson, a St. Louis lawyer who first came to Minnesota in 1854 to get away from the summer heat at home, bought the land in 1870 and built a summer house he called Northome in 1877. In its day people called it the largest home on Lake Minnetonka. It burned in the 1890s, but the stone arch Gibson built at the entrance to his estate is still there, and you can still drive through it on Northome Road. Decades later, the Littles' Wright house on the same point carried the Northome name too.

Gibson was also behind the Hotel St. Louis, which went up in 1879 above nearby St. Louis Bay as the first of the great Lake Minnetonka hotels. In 1900, Northome was one of four summer neighborhoods, along with Deephaven, Cottagewood and Summerville, that voted at Arthur Dyer's boat works to form the village of Deephaven. Cottagewood's story is on the Carson's Bay page.

Today the bay is shared by two small towns: Deephaven on the south and west, and Woodland on the north. Just inland from the bay sits little Lake Marion, which isn't connected to Lake Minnetonka at all.

If you are reading from another state, here is the picture that helps. Lake Minnetonka is a long chain of bays in the western suburbs of Minneapolis, split into an Upper Lake to the west and a Lower Lake to the east. Robinson's Bay is a quiet pocket on the east side of the Lower Lake, south of Wayzata Bay and the town of Wayzata.
