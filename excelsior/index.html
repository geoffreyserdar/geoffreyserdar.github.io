How This Town Started

Before Water Street was Water Street, people came for the water.

I walk this downtown a lot. You can feel how small the city is, and how long people have been arguing about keeping it that way.

George Bertram and the Excelsior Pioneer Association started building here in 1853, after Governor Alexander Ramsey named Lake Minnetonka the year before. It’s widely called the lake’s first village. The village incorporated in 1877.

What stuck around is the bones of a Victorian lake town. The Excelsior Commercial Historic District along Water Street sits on the National Register (SHPO/NPS paperwork dated September 3, 2021), about 4.58 acres, mostly contributing buildings from the 1886 to 1955 stretch. Locally, the downtown historic district was designated by the City Council in December 1998. You’re not buying a suburb that happens to sit near water. You’re buying into a place that decided, a long time ago, what it wanted to look like.

When the town was first platted, the lakeside Commons were set aside as a public pleasure ground, those thirteen acres are still public. Streetcar service opened in 1906 and ended in 1932. If you want the longer story, Danceland, the amusement park that ran 1925 to 1973, the Minnesota Streetcar Museum line that still runs restored cars here. We can talk about it when we walk. The short version for a buyer: history isn’t a brochure here. It shows up in lot lines, setbacks, and what you’re allowed to change.

Bays & Shoreline

Excelsior Bay is the water right off downtown. St. Albans Bay and Gideon’s Bay are just around the shore.

People say “Lake Minnetonka” like it’s one room. It isn’t. Excelsior sits on the south shore, looking into Excelsior Bay, the water you see from the Commons and the Port. That’s the bay that makes downtown feel like it belongs to the lake.

Just east, St. Albans Bay runs between Excelsior and Greenwood. Minnetonka Boulevard crosses the channel on the St. Albans Bay Bridge; the cities of Excelsior and Greenwood jointly manage that connection, and property owners with access rights sit under the St. Alban’s Bay Lake Improvement District (SABLID), set up in 2015 as the first LID on Lake Minnetonka. Private marina docks sit on both Excelsior Bay and St. Albans Bay a few blocks from downtown, separate from the city’s own residential mooring program.

Around the corner of the south shore, Gideon’s Bay is shared with Shorewood and Tonka Bay, and Deephaven sits in that same general belt. It’s the same lake, but the week-to-week feel changes with the bay. If you’re shopping “Excelsior,” I want to know whether you mean a walk-to-Water-Street lot, a St. Albans shoreline address with a Greenwood mailing quirk, or something that only feels Excelsior because that’s where you get dinner. Those are three different houses.

Neighborhoods & Housing

It’s a small city with Victorian bones, and not much wasted land.

Excelsior is tiny by metro standards, about 0.63 square miles of land, and 2,355 people at the 2020 census. That density is the point. You get walkable blocks, older homes with porches that face the street, and newer work tucked into a place that already had a personality.

Downtown is Water Street and the commercial historic core. Around it you’ll find Victorian-era houses that owners have been restoring for decades, plus condominiums and townhomes that showed up later for people who want lake-town life without mowing a big lot. Lakefront and near-lake parcels sit on Excelsior Bay and toward St. Albans; inland streets climb away from the water toward Oak Street and the school.

On age: ACS summaries put roughly a quarter of Excelsior’s housing stock as pre-1940, with a thinner slice built since 2010: numbers move with the survey vintage, so I treat them as a shape, not a listing promise.

What I tell buyers: measure the walk. Can you get to the Commons, a coffee, and the elementary without getting in the car every time? In Excelsior that question actually matters, because the whole town is short. In a bigger west-metro city it’s theater.

Lake Access

The Commons is how a lot of people get on the lake without owning a slip.

The Excelsior Commons and Port of Excelsior are a 13-acre public park that dates to the city’s origins. The city calls them chief assets, and they’re not wrong. You get open lawn, picnic sites, playgrounds, two swimming beaches, tennis, baseball, a band shell, a bathhouse, restrooms, docks, buoys, and docking for public excursion boats, lake access for people who don’t own a boat. Summer concerts land at the bandshell. Art Fair and the Fourth of July take over the lawn.

Separately, the city runs a residential mooring program, docks, buoys, slides, and canoe/kayak racks, under a mooring policy adopted December 5, 2022. Renewals come first; then Excelsior residents in wait-list order. The city says there are typically about five spaces that open each year, though turnover varies. The resident dock waitlist was revised June 10, 2025. Dock slips in that program max out at 24 feet length and 11 feet width; sailboats and certain craft are steered to buoys and slides instead. Buying a house in Excelsior does not hand you a city slip. That’s a second conversation, and I’d rather have it before you fall in love with a porch.

Market Context

I won’t invent a median for a town this small.

Excelsior doesn’t trade like a big suburb. Some weeks you’ll see a couple of closed sales; some weeks you’ll see none. One month in a town this small can swing hard, so I treat a single median as a snapshot, not a promise.

Per the Minneapolis Area REALTORS® Local Market Update for Excelsior, the median sold price in August 2026 was $930,000 (data as of September 8, 2026; NorthstarMLS). That’s what homes actually sold for that month, a sold median, not a list-price number. The update doesn’t publish a citywide median list price, so I won’t invent one.

Separately, because the city published it: the residential dock program typically opens about five spaces a year (waitlist revised June 10, 2025). That’s just how the waitlist works, not a scare line. Buying a house here still doesn’t hand you a city slip, and Excelsior doesn’t advertise a city trailer ramp, if you need to launch with a trailer, we map a nearby regional access and verify it.

Schools & Daily Life

Minnetonka Schools, a downtown you can walk, and a lawn that belongs to everyone.

Kids here go to Minnetonka Public Schools. Excelsior Elementary sits at 441 Oak Street, English, Chinese Immersion, and the district’s Navigator accelerated pathway all live in that building. Middle and high school follow the Minnetonka district map; I always double-check an address against the district before anyone treats a listing blurb as gospel.

Day-to-day life runs on Water Street restaurants and shops, the Commons lawn, the Port, and the streetcar museum line for guests who want to see how this place used to move. Highway 7 and Minnetonka Boulevard carry you toward the rest of the south lake and into the west metro. You’re close to Shorewood, Greenwood, Deephaven, Tonka Bay, and, a short hop across the map, Wayzata on the north shore. Same lake, different downtowns.
