Excelsior this weekend smells like Apple Days. Water Street’s full, and outside Leipold’s there’s a new bench with two names on it — LaVerna and Darel.

She passed in December. They opened that shop in 1971 and it still feels like the same room. Local historians. The kind of people who helped bring Apple Days back in the mid-80s and then just kept showing up for this town, year after year.

Tomorrow morning at 9:30 they’re dedicating the bench right there on Water Street, in front of the shop. If you’re downtown today for pie or the bands, walk past. Sit a minute. Say their names out loud if you want.

Sep 11, 2026 GMB
Lake MinnetonkaExcelsiorCarver County

If you own a home near Lake Minnetonka, you already understand something that numbers alone cannot fully capture. There is a reason people search for years to land here, and once they do, many never leave.

I work with buyers and sellers across Excelsior, Wayzata, Orono, Mound, Minnetrista, Tonka Bay, and the Carver County communities of Chanhassen, Chaska, Victoria, and Waconia. One theme I hear consistently from long-term homeowners is that they are glad they held on. The west metro has a way of rewarding patience.

What makes this area different as a long-term asset comes down to a few things that simply cannot be manufactured elsewhere. Lake Minnetonka is finite. You cannot build more of it. The communities surrounding it have character that took generations to develop: walkable downtowns, genuine neighborhoods, access to water and trails and excellent schools. That combination of scarcity and lifestyle does a lot of heavy lifting when it comes to sustained desirability over time.

Carver County communities like Waconia and Victoria offer their own version of this story: strong community identity, room to grow, and proximity to everything the broader west metro provides.

Holding a home here is not just an emotional decision. It is often a sound long-term one.
