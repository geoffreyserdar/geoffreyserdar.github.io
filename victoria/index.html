Who You're Calling

A hunter, not a farmer.

That's been my line for years — I'd rather go find you the right house than sit and wait for one to show up.

In Victoria, it needs a footnote.

This town was built by farmers. The Diethelm brothers put up the first shelter in 1851, and families came from Germany, Holland and Switzerland on word of mouth about the soil. Wheat was the cash crop. Potatoes, carrots, cabbage and corn fed the families that grew them. The town got its name in 1856, when families on opposite sides of Lake Bavaria settled their differences and the north shore donated the land for a church.

And one of those farmers changed the country. Wendelin Grimm arrived in 1859 carrying seed from Germany he called “everlasting clover.” He spent fifteen winters keeping seed only from the plants that lived through them — and grew the first truly winter-hardy alfalfa in North America. Every modern variety in this country came out of that field.

His farmstead is still standing, inside Carver Park Reserve. The road it sits on carries his name.

So — hunter, not farmer. But I know whose ground this is.

What Victoria Actually Is

The City of Lakes and Parks — and it means it.

Victoria calls itself the City of Lakes and Parks, which is the rare case of a city slogan being an understatement. Carver Park Reserve alone holds 604 acres of water and three fishable lakes — Stieger, Auburn and Zumbra — plus 25 miles of hiking trail, 10 miles of mountain-bike singletrack, cross-country ski and horseback trails, a campground, and the Lowry Nature Center, the first public nature center ever built in the Twin Cities.

The Minnesota Landscape Arboretum is here. So is the Wassermann Lake Preserve. The Lake Minnetonka LRT Regional Trail runs more than fifteen miles from Hopkins straight through town and into the park reserve — you can ride to Excelsior for lunch without touching a highway.

Kids attend Eastern Carver County Schools (District 112). Downtown is small and walkable, Bayfront Park sits on Stieger Lake, and you are twenty-five minutes from a Minneapolis office and about four minutes from a trailhead.

Choosing Victoria over Excelsior or Shorewood. In Excelsior or Shorewood you are buying a Lake Minnetonka address, and the address is a large part of the price. Victoria trades that for room — generally more house and more lot for the same money, District 112 schools, and Carver Park Reserve on the edge of town rather than a dock on the water. Here the lakes are somewhere you go, not something you pay a premium to look at. Both are good answers. They are just answers to different questions, and it is worth knowing which one you are asking before you start touring.
